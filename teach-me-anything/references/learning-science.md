# Learning-science basis

These sources inform the teaching design. They do not establish that an AI
tutor, a particular question count, or this complete workflow has been validated.
Use source-linked summaries, not copied textbook passages.

## Prior knowledge, goals, and context

**National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2018).**
*How People Learn II: Learners, Contexts, and Cultures.*
[Summary and conclusions](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/2).
DOI: 10.17226/24783.

Prior knowledge can support new learning and also bias reasoning. Learning
strategies depend on the learner, material, and goal. Meaningful goals, agency,
and monitoring one's learning matter.

Design implications: diagnose prerequisites and misconceptions; agree on useful
capabilities; adapt from observed responses rather than assigning a fixed
"learning style."

## Retrieval, spacing, and elaboration

**Weinstein, Y., Madan, C. R., and Sumeracki, M. A. (2018).**
*Teaching the science of learning.* Cognitive Research: Principles and
Implications, 3, 2.
[Open-access review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5780548/).
DOI: 10.1186/s41235-017-0087-y.

The review discusses retrieval practice, spaced practice, interleaving,
elaboration, concrete examples, and dual coding. Longer-term retention is not
the same as fluent performance immediately after instruction.

Design implications: retrieve before rereading, revisit earlier material, ask
why and how, connect abstractions to examples, and use relevant diagrams with
explanations. Mix related problem types when the learner has enough foundation;
do not confuse interleaving with arbitrary topic switching.

Spacing supports distributed opportunities to learn. It does not require
attendance on fixed days. This skill uses actual returns and performance to
choose reviews, and does not infer a precise forgetting curve from a timestamp.

## Worked examples and explanatory questions

**Pashler, H., and colleagues (2007).**
*Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning.*
Institute of Education Sciences practice guide.
[Recommendations and evidence levels](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/PracticeGuide/1).

The guide recommends spacing, alternating worked solutions and problem solving,
combining graphics with verbal descriptions, connecting abstract and concrete
representations, quizzing, and deep explanatory questions. Its recommendations
have different evidence ratings; they should not all be described as equally
well established.

Design implications: model reasoning for novices and fade assistance toward
independence. Ask for explanation and application, not only recognition.

## Informative feedback

**Wisniewski, B., Zierer, K., and Hattie, J. (2020).**
*The Power of Feedback Revisited: A Meta-Analysis of Educational Feedback Research.*
[Open-access article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087/full).
DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087.

The meta-analysis found substantial variability in feedback effects, with
information content an important moderator. "Feedback works" is too broad a
prescription.

Design implication: identify the specific gap and a useful next action. Bare
grades, generic praise, and repeated explanations are not sufficient repair.

## Three successes and delayed relearning

**Rawson, K. A., and Dunlosky, J. (2011).**
*Optimizing Schedules of Retrieval Practice for Durable and Efficient Learning:
How Much Is Enough?* Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 140(3), 283-302.
[Abstract in ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ934616).
DOI: 10.1037/a0023956.

Across three experiments with 533 students learning conceptual material,
the authors recommended an initial criterion of three correct recalls followed
by relearning three times at widely spaced intervals.

This is not evidence that three consecutive answers on arbitrary mathematical
or technical tasks prove comprehensive understanding. The skill's consecutive,
independent, varied-success rule is an explicit product policy. Delayed checks
provide additional evidence under the conditions and dates tested.

## Design choices, not universal research constants

The coverage-first adaptive placement, five-round/twenty-question ceilings,
early-stop rules, three-success streak, default evidence variety, one-to-three
re-entry probes, phase gates, and review priorities are practical choices.
The adaptive placement is not a calibrated psychometric test. Adjust scope and
challenge using the learner's goal and demonstrated performance. Do not market
these numbers as scientifically optimal.

Neither a short diagnostic nor a high-confidence answer establishes complete
mastery. Transfer must be assessed rather than assumed, and success in one
context should not be generalized without evidence.
